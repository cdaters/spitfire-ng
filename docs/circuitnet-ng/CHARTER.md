# Proposed CircuitNET NG Charter

**Draft for participating Sysop review. No policy is adopted by this document.**

## Purpose and participation

CircuitNET NG is a hobbyist association of independent BBS Sysops sharing useful,
welcoming conference discussion. Participating software may be independently
implemented if it conforms to the published protocol and network policy. SPITFIRE
NG is one implementation, not a requirement to surrender local BBS authority.

Each Sysop operates and moderates their BBS, identifies a working administrative
contact, protects node credentials, and honors published conference and routing
agreements. A node joins through an agreed HOST, unique Node ID, explicit tree
placement and tested authenticated link. Connection direction does not determine
network role. An END may poll from behind a firewall.

## Network roles and offices

The ROOT/network administrator maintains the agreed tree and coordinates changes.
HOST Sysops service their direct children and arrange dependable exchanges. END
Sysops maintain their upstream relationship and local conference mappings.
An Operations Committee of participating Sysops advises on reliability, conduct,
conference lifecycle and disputed decisions. A Secretary maintains dated decisions,
proposals, contact procedures and published changes. Conference moderators guide
topic and conduct and communicate with affected Sysops. Offices should be small,
practical volunteer responsibilities; they confer no ownership of other boards.

## Decisions and conference lifecycle

Any participating Sysop may propose a conference, retirement, merge, rename or rule
change through the published administrative channel. Proposals describe purpose,
codename, audience, moderation and operational effects. Allow a stated discussion
period and publish the decision and effective date. Ordinary decisions seek
consensus; unresolved network-wide changes may use an announced electronic Sysop
vote with one participating node's designated Sysop voice, a clear eligible list,
quorum and closing time agreed before voting. The Secretary retains the result.
The initial participation, office selection and quorum rules require review before
this draft becomes operational; the software does not implement voting authority.

Codenames are stable network identities, independent of local conference numbers.
Merges and removals need migration notice and an archival explanation. No committee
proposal creates a conference on a BBS automatically. Sysops explicitly map and
subscribe through their HOST's approved Dossier policy.

## Reliability and inactive nodes

Publish expected poll frequency and administrative contact methods. After missed
service, a HOST contacts the Sysop before classifying a node inactive. Temporarily
hold delivery when necessary to protect storage or the network, preserve durable
queue truth, and agree a resumption or retirement plan. Do not silently reassign a
Node ID while old identity or queued traffic could be confused with its replacement.

## Conduct, disputes and emergency action

Moderators first address problems with the affected Sysops using minimal necessary
content and an opportunity to respond. Repeated harassment, malicious content or
routing abuse may justify a conference restriction, delivery hold or suspension.
Record the reason, scope, duration and review contact. A network-wide removal should
receive Operations Committee review; an urgent security hold may precede that
review. Appeals go to uninvolved committee members. Publish useful policy decisions
without exposing private contact information or unnecessarily redistributing abuse.

## Privacy and security

CircuitNET NG transport between configured nodes is encrypted and authenticated.
Public conference messages are not end-to-end encrypted. After delivery they are
readable according to each destination BBS's access rules. Directed routing does
not make a conference message private. Local messages called private, where offered,
are access-restricted unless a separate feature explicitly provides end-to-end
cryptography. Do not post secrets to conference traffic. Node administrators must
maintain credentials, backups, updates, and truthful incident communication.

Changes to this Charter follow the same published proposal and review process.
The network exists for its participants; administration should remain understandable
and proportionate to a volunteer BBS community.
